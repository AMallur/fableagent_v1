# HIPAA Security Officer Designation

**Citation:** 45 CFR §164.308(a)(2) — the Security Rule requires a covered
entity or business associate to identify the security official responsible
for developing and implementing its security policies and procedures.

---

Effective **[DATE]**, **[YOUR FULL NAME]** is designated the **HIPAA Security
Officer** for **[COMPANY LEGAL NAME]** ("the Company"), a business associate
providing revenue-cycle-management software and services to healthcare
covered entities.

## Responsibilities

The Security Officer is responsible for:

1. Developing, maintaining, and enforcing the Company's HIPAA Security Rule
   policies and procedures (see `compliance/policies/`).
2. Conducting and periodically updating the organizational risk analysis
   (`compliance/risk_analysis.md`), at minimum annually or after any material
   change to the Company's systems, infrastructure, or PHI-handling
   processes.
3. Overseeing workforce security-awareness training and maintaining
   completion records (`compliance/workforce_training_log.csv`).
4. Receiving, investigating, and documenting all suspected or confirmed
   security incidents and PHI breaches, and executing the breach-notification
   procedure (`compliance/policies/incident_response_and_breach_notification_policy.md`)
   when required.
5. Reviewing and approving Business Associate Agreements with the Company's
   subprocessors (hosting/storage, email, clearinghouse) and with the
   Company's own healthcare-provider clients.
6. Reviewing access-control changes and conducting periodic access reviews
   for any system that can reach PHI (production database, document storage,
   AWS account, CI/CD deploy credentials).
7. Serving as the point of contact for HIPAA compliance questions from
   clients, auditors, and regulators.

## Until this changes

This designation remains in effect until superseded by a dated, signed
replacement. As the Company hires additional workforce members, this
designation should be reviewed to confirm it still reflects who actually
holds operational security responsibility.
